Beuk and Atticus

Atticus and Beuk are both tools for writing and formatting a book that run on more than one kind of computer. The clearest difference today is the price: Atticus has one, paid once.

Every fact about Atticus on this page comes from Atticus's own website and is linked in the sources below, checked 26 October 2026. Prices and features change, so check their site before you decide.

In short

Atticus may suit you if…

you want to pay once, with a published price and a money-back guarantee, for a writing and formatting tool on any computer.

Beuk may suit you if…

you want to start without paying or signing up, and want store presets, store checks, Story Insights and beta-reader links in the same studio.

Where Atticus is stronger

  • A one-time price. Atticus is $147, paid once, with ongoing updates. [1] Writing and exporting on your device need no Beuk account; Beuk's account features have a 7-day free trial, then one price that is not announced yet.
  • A money-back guarantee. Atticus offers 30 days. [1]
  • PDF export. Atticus lists PDF among its exports. [1] Beuk's print PDF is made with your browser's Print → Save as PDF.
  • More templates. Atticus lists 17; Beuk has fifteen themes. [1]

Where Beuk is different

  • No account to start. Writing on your device never needs one, and export stays open whatever your account's state.
  • Eleven EPUB store presets, plus a Store-ready? report against KDP's and IngramSpark's own print rules.
  • Story Insights and History: the book at a glance, counted on your device, and snapshots that show what changed since each one.
  • Beta-reader links, with an account, where readers comment by the paragraph.
  • Narrator scripts in ACX order for an audiobook.

What Beuk doesn't do

  • It has no published price yet, and no one-time purchase has been announced.
  • Without an account it does not make a PDF itself: the print PDF comes from your browser's Print → Save as PDF. With an account, Beuk's server converts the print interior to PDF/X-1a.
  • It has no plotting board, no real-time co-authoring and no track changes.
