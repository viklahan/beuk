A printer cannot print exactly to the edge of a page. To get a picture that runs right off it, the page is printed a little larger and trimmed back. That extra is the bleed.

## Steps
1. Turn on **Bleed**. Beuk's print-ready PDF then has pages 0.125 in larger on the top, the bottom and the outer edge, the way KDP and IngramSpark ask for a book with pictures to the edge. A 6 × 9 in book prints at 6.125 × 9.25 in.
   say: Turn on bleed. The print file's pages grow an eighth of an inch on the top, the bottom and the outer edge, the size K D P and IngramSpark ask for. A six by nine inch book becomes six and an eighth by nine and a quarter.
   do: show "Bleed"
2. Full-page plates and spread backdrops run out into the bleed, so the printer trims through the picture instead of leaving a white sliver. The words, the running heads and the page numbers do not move.
   say: Full-page pictures and spread backdrops run out into that extra space, so the trim cuts through the picture, not beside it. The text stays exactly where it was.
   do: show "Bleed"
3. Bleed is off until you turn it on. If your book has full-page plates or backdrops, the print settings and the store check suggest it. The print view shows the bleed shaded beyond a dashed trim line.
   say: Bleed stays off until you turn it on, and Beuk suggests it when your book has full-page pictures. In the print view, you will see it shaded beyond a dashed trim line.
   do: show "Bleed"

## Takeaway
No pictures to the edge, no bleed. A novel of plain text pages does not need it.
