Before a book goes to a printer, someone should look at every page. The print view lets you, and preflight checks what is easy to miss.

## Steps
1. **Open Print View** to see the pages laid out as they will print, spread by spread. It is a preview, for checking the pages and printing them yourself.
   say: Open the print view and turn through the pages, spread by spread, the way they will print. This is a preview, for your eyes and your own printer.
   do: show "Open Print View"
2. **Run Print Preflight**. It checks the margins and the gutter against the printer's minimums for your page count and trim, every picture's resolution at the size it will print, the page count against KDP's limits for that trim, and lists the fonts in use.
   say: Then run print preflight. It checks the margins against the printer's minimums, every picture's resolution at the size it will print, and the page count against K D P's limits, and lists the fonts you are using.
   do: show "Run Print Preflight"
3. Fix what preflight flags, then run it again. A low-resolution picture is the most common one: replace it with a larger file.
   say: Fix whatever it flags, and run it again. The one you will see most is a picture that is too small to print sharply. The cure is a larger file.
   do: show "Run Print Preflight"

## Takeaway
Preflight catches the problem here, in a minute, instead of at the printer, in a week.
