The front cover makes a browser pick the book up; the back cover makes them keep it. In the wrap it carries its own copy and its own look.

## Steps
1. In the Cover Maker's Print Wrap section, write the **Back-cover blurb** — the selling copy a browser reads in the shop.
   say: In the Print Wrap section, write the back-cover blurb: the selling copy a browser reads in the shop.
   do: click "Project Settings"; wait 1; click "Make a Cover"; wait 2; show "Back-cover blurb"
2. Add a **Praise / quote** line if you have one, and an **Imprint line** for the foot of the cover.
   say: Add a line of praise if you have one, and an imprint line for the foot of the cover.
   do: show "Praise / quote"; show "Imprint line"
3. The **Back cover** section gives the back a look of its own: a colour, a gradient or a pattern behind it, with its own font and colour. On a dust jacket, the back's colour reaches across the fold, so the wrap reads as one piece.
   say: The Back cover section gives the back a look of its own: a colour, a gradient or a pattern behind it, with its own font and colour. On a dust jacket, the back's colour reaches across the fold.
   do: show "Back cover"

## Takeaway
A browser gives the back cover ten seconds: a blurb that earns them, one line of praise, and an imprint that says somebody stands behind the book. Write it as carefully as the first page.
