On a bookshop shelf the spine is the whole advertisement. In Beuk its width is not a guess: it is worked out from your page count and the paper the printer uses.

## Steps
1. In the Cover Maker, the **Print Wrap** section lays out the whole paperback cover — back, spine and front. Give it your **Trim size** and **Page count**, and pick a **Paper stock**: KDP White at 0.0025 in per page, KDP Cream at 0.0027, or Custom for another printer's spec. The spine's width follows.
   say: The Print Wrap section lays out the whole paperback cover: back, spine and front. Give it your trim size and page count, and pick a paper stock. The spine's width follows from those.
   do: click "Project Settings"; wait 1; click "Make a Cover"; wait 2; show "Paper stock"
2. The **Spine** section gives it words of its own, its own background, font and colour, and an icon at the head and the foot of the spine. A long imprint at the foot shrinks to fit instead of being cut off.
   say: The Spine section gives it words of its own, its own background, font and colour, and an icon at the head and the foot. A long imprint shrinks to fit instead of being cut off.
   do: show "Spine"
3. Direction matters: English, Dutch and Nordic spines read top to bottom, the way those shops shelve them; other books keep bottom to top — and you can choose either.
   say: Direction matters. English, Dutch and Nordic spines read top to bottom, the way those shops shelve them. Other books keep bottom to top, and you can choose either.
   do: show "Spine"
4. A thin book gets an honest answer: with a spine under half an inch, the text stays off and the spine stays plain — printers recommend exactly that.
   say: A thin book gets an honest answer. With a spine under half an inch, the text stays off and the spine stays plain. Printers recommend exactly that.
   do: show "Page count"

## Takeaway
The spine is computed, not drawn by eye: trim, pages and paper decide its width, so the file you upload matches the book the printer binds.
