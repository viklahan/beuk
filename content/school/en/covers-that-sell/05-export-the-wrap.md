One cover, three files: the front for the store catalogue, and the whole wrap, as PDF or JPG, for the printer — at the size the printers themselves publish.

## Steps
1. Proof it first. **Guides** draws the trim, fold and safety lines over the wrap preview, and the dimensions line states the exact size being made.
   say: Proof it first. Guides draws the trim, fold and safety lines over the wrap preview, and the dimensions line states the exact size being made.
   do: click "Project Settings"; wait 1; click "Make a Cover"; wait 2; show "Guides"
2. The stand below shows the bound book in three dimensions — drag to spin it, and read the spine the way a browser in a shop would.
   say: The stand below shows the bound book in three dimensions. Drag to spin it, and read the spine the way a browser in a shop would.
   do: show "Drag to spin"
3. For the ebook and the catalogue, download the front alone: **Ebook JPG · 2560 × 1600**, or **Print JPG · 300 DPI** for print-resolution art. **Use as My Cover** writes it into the book itself, so the EPUB carries it from then on.
   say: For the ebook and the catalogue, download the front alone, as an ebook J P G, or a print-resolution one. Use as My Cover writes it into the book itself.
   do: show "Ebook JPG"
4. For the printer, **Download wrap PDF** or **Download wrap JPG**. The wrap is built on Beuk's server from your book, so you need to be signed in and online, and the PDF comes out at the exact trim-plus-bleed size KDP Print, IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble Press, Lulu and BookVault ask for.
   say: For the printer, download the wrap P D F or J P G. The wrap is built on Beuk's server from your book, so you need to be signed in and online, and the file comes out at the exact size the print-on-demand printers ask for.
   do: show "Download wrap PDF"

## Takeaway
The front sells the ebook; the wrap sells the paperback. Proof on the stand, then upload the server's wrap file — never a screenshot of the preview.
