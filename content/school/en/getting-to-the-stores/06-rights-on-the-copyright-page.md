title: Rights on the copyright page
blurb: All rights reserved, public domain or Creative Commons — chosen once in Project Settings, written honestly into every format.
---
The copyright page makes a claim, and not every book should claim everything. An annotated classic isn't yours to reserve all rights to; a book you want shared freely shouldn't pretend otherwise. Beuk writes the notice you actually mean.

## Steps
1. Open **Project Settings** and find **Rights**. The default is **All rights reserved**, unchanged from what every book claimed before; the other choices are **Public domain**, **Creative Commons** and **Custom**.
   say: Open Project Settings and find Rights. The default is All rights reserved, unchanged. The other choices are Public domain, Creative Commons, and Custom.
   do: click "Project Settings"; wait 1; show "Rights"
2. A public-domain book's copyright page and EPUB say "This work is in the public domain.", with the original publication year — and, if you give them, a "This edition ©" line for the edition's own additions, your introduction or notes. No line claims all rights reserved.
   say: A public domain book's copyright page and EPUB say, this work is in the public domain, with the original publication year. And, if you give them, a this edition line for the edition's own additions. No line claims all rights reserved.
   do: show "Public domain"
3. **Creative Commons** names the licence you pick — CC BY, BY-SA, BY-NC, BY-NC-SA, BY-ND or CC0 — together with its web address, so a reader can look the terms up.
   say: Creative Commons names the licence you pick, together with its web address, so a reader can look the terms up.
   do: show "Creative Commons"
4. One choice, every format: print, EPUB, Markdown and Word all write the same notice. And books you already have are untouched — they print exactly as before.
   say: One choice, every format. Print, EPUB, Markdown and Word all write the same notice. And books you already have are untouched. They print exactly as before.
   do: show "Rights"

## Takeaway
Rights are a setting, not boilerplate: say once what the book's terms are, and every edition's copyright page tells the same truth.
