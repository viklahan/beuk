Some copies are for one pair of eyes: the editor, the agent, the printer. Three of Beuk's exports can be downloaded locked, so the file opens only for the person holding the password.

## Steps
1. In Export, under **Word Document (.docx)**, turn on **Lock with a password**, type the password twice, and download as usual. Word itself will ask your editor for the password when they open it.
   say: In Export, under the Word document, turn on Lock with a password, type it twice, and download as usual. Word itself asks your editor for the password when they open the file.
   do: show "Word Document"; show "Lock with a password"
2. The same lock sits on **Prepare Submission Manuscript**, for the copy that goes to an agent or a publisher.
   say: The same lock sits on the submission manuscript, for the copy that goes out to an agent or a publisher.
   do: show "Prepare Submission Manuscript"
3. For the print-ready PDF, choose **Download locked copy**. The PDF is locked with 256-bit AES encryption.
   say: And for the print-ready P D F, choose Download locked copy. The P D F is locked with two hundred and fifty six bit A E S encryption.
   do: show "Download locked copy"
4. The lock is made in your browser and the password never reaches Beuk — without it, nobody can open the file, Beuk included. Send the password separately, by text or a call, not in the same email as the file. The password boxes empty themselves whenever the dialog closes.
   say: The lock is made in your browser, and the password never reaches Beuk. Send it separately, by text or a call, not in the same email as the file.
   do: show "Lock with a password"

## Takeaway
A locked copy travels like any attachment and opens like a safe. Without a password, your downloads are exactly as before.
