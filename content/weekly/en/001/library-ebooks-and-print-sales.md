---
title: A Publishers’ Study Says Library Ebooks Cost Print Sales
section: The Money Desk
place: short
order: 1
---
The AAP and the Authors Guild [released a study](https://publishers.org/news/new-study-analyzes-the-impact-of-library-e-lending-on-commercial-book-markets/) on September 1 that they commissioned from economists at Secretariat Advisors. It finds that consumer print sales fall “approximately 0.85 to 1.0 percent for every one-percentage-point increase in the share of e-books in public libraries,” with adult fiction at the upper end, and that ebooks now make up nearly half of many library collections.

The groups use it against state laws that cap e-licence prices, while saying that “fully funding libraries should be an urgent priority for all lawmakers.”

:: This is one side’s evidence, paid for by two parties to the debate. Expect it in every statehouse fight over library ebook terms, which set what wide authors earn through library distribution.
