A reader decides in the first line of a chapter whether to keep going. The type around that line can help it, or get in its way.

## The drop cap

A large initial letter tells the eye where the chapter begins. It is the oldest signal in the printed book, and in Beuk it is a switch on each chapter's page. Use it on chapters, not on every section, or it stops meaning anything.

## The small-caps lead-in

Setting the first few words in small capitals carries the reader from the drop cap into the sentence. It works best when those words are worth slowing down for: a name, a place, the line the chapter turns on.

## The opening plate

A full-page picture just before a chapter sets the scene before a word is read. Beuk asks for alt text with every plate, so a reader who cannot see the picture still knows what it shows.

> Openings are set per chapter, so a prologue can look different from chapter one, and it usually should.

None of this rescues a weak first line. It only makes sure a strong one is seen.
