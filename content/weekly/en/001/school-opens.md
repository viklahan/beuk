The first course at Beuk School is called Maiden Voyage, and it is the whole studio in twelve short lessons. It begins where every book in Beuk begins, on the desk, and ends with a file that has been read the way a store will read it.

The lessons run from under half a minute to just under a minute each. They open on the sample novella, *The Lantern Coast*, so a writer can follow along on the same book, or on their own in another tab.

Each lesson comes three ways: a narrated screen recording with chapters, the same lesson as audio with the full transcript beneath it, and written steps with a screenshot for every one. All of it is free.

## Filmed, not staged

No lesson was mocked up. A recorder drives the live studio through each step: it moves a pointer to the control, rings it, presses it, and holds the screen for exactly as long as the narration takes to describe what happened.

> If a lesson names a button the studio no longer shows, the recording fails and the lesson is not published.

That rule is the point. A course about software goes stale the week the software changes. This one cannot quietly describe a control that is gone, because it is recorded against the real thing every time.

## Three ways in

The narration is an AI voice, and every lesson page says so. Writers who sign in can mark each lesson done, and Beuk remembers it; writers who don't are welcome all the same, and nothing about them is kept.

## What comes next

Maiden Voyage is the first course. More are being written, and each will be announced here first.
