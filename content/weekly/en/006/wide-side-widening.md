Rakuten Kobo switched on Kobo Plus in Estonia, Lithuania and Slovenia on October 28: €9.99 a month for Read (“millions of e-books”) or Listen (“hundreds of thousands of audiobooks”), €12.99 for both, with a commitment-free 14-day trial, on the Kobo apps and every Kobo e-reader, [Good e-Reader reports](https://goodereader.com/blog/kobo-ereader-news/kobo-plus-is-now-available-in-estonia-lithuania-and-slovenia). The launch date comes via [Inkfluence AI's roundup](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/self-publishing-news-november-2026), which also notes audiobooks joined the French Kobo Plus tier two weeks earlier, on October 15.

The cadence is the story. Country by country, tier by tier, Kobo has spent the year widening a subscription program whose terms an author can read on one page: the price, the trial, the enrollment toggle in Kobo Writing Life or an aggregator dashboard. No undisclosed thresholds, no funds of unannounced size.

That transparency is worth naming in a week when the other subscription story — the continuing fallout from Amazon's Kindle+ transition, covered below — still turns on numbers nobody outside Seattle has seen. An author deciding where a catalog lives deserves arithmetic that can be checked.

:: If you distribute wide through Kobo Writing Life or an aggregator, your subscription footprint just grew three countries without you lifting a finger. Confirm your enrollment and territories — and let this stand as the measuring stick you hold every platform to: terms you can read, growth you can count.
