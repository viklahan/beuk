Penguin Random House is remediating its entire list — tens of thousands of backlist titles — into accessible ebook editions, and the honest arithmetic is striking: “nearly 10% of our active backlist is currently fully accessible,” PRH's Liisa McCloy-Kelley told [Publishing Perspectives](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/11/every-book-available-to-reader-publishing-perspectives-talks-accessible-ebooks-with-penguin-random-houses-liisa-mccloy-kelley) on November 17, with the majority “several years” away. The target is WCAG 2.1 AA — the bar she says both the European Accessibility Act and US Title II set — with alt text drafted by in-house tools and reviewed by an internal QA team. Her best line doubles as a warning: “Ebooks are essentially software that needs to be updated over time.”

:: One of the world's largest publishers needs years and an undisclosed “significant” budget to retrofit accessibility. An indie with one EPUB can do it at build time: run ACE, write real alt text, declare accessibility metadata — and you are ahead of most of the Random House backlist.
