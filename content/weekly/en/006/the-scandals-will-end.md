title: The Scandals Will End. The Scoring Won't.
section: The Leader · Opinion
place: leader
---
Jane Friedman [predicts the AI-detection scandals will soon be over](https://janefriedman.com/why-the-ai-detection-scandals-will-end-probably-quite-soon/) — not because detection stops, but because it moves upstream. The 2026 blowups, she argues, came from books acquired before detectors were common and published after they were everywhere: “so the scandals of 2026 come from the gap between those two moments.” Soon manuscripts will be quietly scored before acquisition, and surprise public accusations will fade. *We think she is right about the quiet — and that quiet is the part to worry about.* A screen that runs before the offer means the verdict arrives as silence: no accusation to answer, no score to contest, and — as her own commenters attest with pre-AI work flagged in parts — no way to know whether the machine was wrong about you. The working author's defense is unglamorous: keep your drafts, your dates, your revision history. Provenance is becoming the receipt you show when the scorer won't show you its arithmetic.
