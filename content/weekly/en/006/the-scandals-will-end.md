---
title: The Scandals Will End. The Scoring Won't.
section: The Leader · Opinion
place: leader
---
Jane Friedman [predicts the AI-detection scandals will soon be over](https://janefriedman.com/why-the-ai-detection-scandals-will-end-probably-quite-soon/) — not because detection stops, but because it moves ahead of publication. The 2026 blowups, she argues, came from books acquired before detectors were common and published after they were everywhere: “so the scandals of 2026 come from the gap between those two moments.” Manuscripts will be scored before publication, she expects, publishers will pass on the ones whose provenance looks like trouble, and surprise public accusations will fade. *We think she is right about the quiet — and that quiet is the part to worry about.* A screen that runs before the offer means the verdict arrives as silence: no accusation to answer, no score to contest, and — as her own commenters attest with pre-AI work flagged in parts, though she replies that such stories are “often flawed and based on use of free tools and short excerpts” — no way to know whether the machine was wrong about you. The working author's defense is unglamorous: keep your drafts, your dates, your revision history. Provenance is becoming the receipt you show when the scorer won't show you its arithmetic.
