---
title: Amazon Rewires the Royalty Math
section: The Platform Desk
byline: the Beuk Correspondent
deck: Ad reports lose their per-page royalty estimates as the Kindle+ transition keeps authors guessing at their own income
---
On November 11, Amazon quietly updated its Ads support pages with one flat sentence: “Estimated KENP royalties metrics are no longer available in Reporting.” The metric, which since 2020 estimated royalties from every page read after an ad click, is gone, [PPC Land reported](https://ppc.land/amazon-ads-drops-page-based-royalty-estimates-from-kindle-authors-reports/) the next day. In its place: estimated Kindle Unlimited royalties computed from “qualified borrows” — downloads where a reader read enough pages to qualify. How many pages is enough? Amazon does not say.

The replacement creates a contradiction Amazon has not resolved. KDP tells authors they are paid from the first page read; the ad console now speaks of a qualification threshold. The two pages do not reconcile, and since the reading metrics are the only Kindle Unlimited income visible in the ad console at all, authors are left checking borrow-based estimates against page-based payouts by hand. Attribution runs fourteen days after a click, and offline reading can take a week to surface.

This lands on already tender ground. Since a reporting change on October 21, authors have reported KENP figures down 40 to 50 percent — in most cases, [per Inkfluence AI's reconstruction](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/kdp-kenp-page-reads-drop-kindle-plus-2026), because Virtual Voice audiobook listening on Audible Plus was moved out of the KENP count, retroactively, into the Audible royalties column, where it is paid from a separate fund. The next day, Kindle Unlimited became Kindle+, folding eligible audiobooks for US subscribers into the KDP Select Global Fund. Some authors with no Virtual Voice titles also reported drops; those remain unexplained, and KDP support has answered inconsistently, calling the figures a glitch in one chat and correct in the next. September's fund paid out $69.3 million.

Then, on November 13, one more quiet default: Sponsored Products campaigns gained an “Adjust reach on and off Amazon” setting that ships set to “Increase reach off-Amazon,” [per the same outlet's November roundup](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/self-publishing-news-november-2026) — your book ads now travel to creator storefronts and beyond unless you say otherwise.

:: If you advertise into Kindle+, your return-on-ad-spend arithmetic just changed from per-page to per-borrow, with an undisclosed divisor. Compute your own figure — KENP read times the published per-page rate — and save dated copies of every report. The mid-November fund announcement for October, the first to include audiobook listening, is the real test of what this transition pays.
