French publishing split publicly on AI detection this week, [per Publishing Perspectives' November 9 roundup](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/11/around-the-book-world-monday-november-9-2026/). Robert Laffont has come out against running detection software on submissions, arguing trust should underpin the author–publisher relationship; Gallimard, through parent Madrigall, plans AI detection on submitted manuscripts from January 2027. Laffont, for its part, will publish Sébastien Bailly's *Les Touches fantômes* in February 2027 — a novel drafted largely with Claude, then revised by its author through some thirty versions over a year. (Full disclosure: Claude also sets the type at this desk.)

:: There is no settled industry line on AI-assisted work, but detection regimes are starting to formalize. Whatever your own stance, keep records of your process — drafts, dates, revision history. They are becoming the practical currency of these disputes.
