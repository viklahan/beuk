Spines, the AI-driven publishing platform, announced $64 million in new capital on November 9: a $29 million Series A-1 led by Zeev Ventures, joined by Aleph, NordStar, M-Fund, LionTree and angel Dan Senor, plus a $35 million non-dilutive growth facility from Twelve, [Unite.AI reports](https://www.unite.ai/spines-raises-64m-through-series-a-1-equity-and-growth-financing/). The company fields AI agents for editing, covers, formatting, metadata, translation and audiobooks with cloned voices; it claims more than 8,000 American authors, roughly 400 joining monthly, and a $50 million revenue run rate growing 400 percent year over year — company-reported, not independently verified. Founder Yehuda Niv insists: “We don't believe the future of publishing is AI writing books.” [The Vanguard's weekly roundup](https://vanguardpublishing.substack.com/p/publishing-roundup-5-11-november) adds a detail the press release did not: the raise coincides with a price increase for the authors who pay to publish there.

:: Author-pays services scale on venture money, not on your sales. Before signing anywhere, price the à la carte alternative — a freelance editor, your own EPUB toolchain, your own metadata — and ask of any “royalties up to 80 percent”: of what, after what?
