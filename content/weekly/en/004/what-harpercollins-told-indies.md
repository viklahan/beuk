---
title: What HarperCollins Just Told Every Indie
section: Money & Margins
byline: the Beuk Correspondent
deck: A nine-percent quarter built on audiobooks and backlist — the two levers that don't need a corporation to pull
---
HarperCollins opened its fiscal 2027 with revenue up 9 percent to $582 million and EBITDA up 29 percent to $79 million, according to Publishers Weekly figures carried in [Publishing Perspectives' November 9 roundup](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/11/around-the-book-world-monday-november-9-2026/). Digital sales grew 6 percent; audiobook revenue grew 13 percent, which parent News Corp credited to its Spotify partnership.

The number worth taping over an indie author's desk is neither of those. Backlist titles made up 66 percent of consumer revenue — two-thirds of the business came from books that are not new. The frontlist makes the headlines; the backlist pays the rent.

News Corp chief executive Robert Thomson described the company's AI posture as double-handed: defending its intellectual property against unauthorized use while pursuing AI productivity gains inside the house.

:: The two engines of that quarter — audio flowing through subscription channels, and a deep backlist that keeps selling — are exactly the levers an indie can pull without a corporation: distribute your audio wide, and tend the backlist (fresh metadata, series links, covers that still look current) instead of living launch to launch.
