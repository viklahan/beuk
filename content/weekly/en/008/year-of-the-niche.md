Rakuten Kobo's 2026 Book Report, released November 10, reads like a funeral for the monoculture bestseller. “This year marked a departure from the notion of 'one book for everyone' bestsellers,” CEO Michael Tamblyn said, [per Good e-Reader's write-up](https://goodereader.com/blog/kobo-ereader-news/rakuten-kobo-released-their-2026-book-report). BookTok energy moved from single viral titles into subgenres — “sad girl lit,” cozy fantasy, romantasy subtropes — while sports romance dominated charts in Canada, the US, Australia, Italy and Spain, and classics like *The Odyssey* and *Wuthering Heights* sat on bestseller lists beside new releases. Page-to-screen pull was real: *Project Hail Mary* was the US bestselling title of the year on the film's success. Kobo's 2027 forecast: a folk horror surge, a dystopian resurgence, trad-life fiction following breakout *Yesteryear*, and romantasy hybridizing with political intrigue, myth retellings and mystery.

:: Readers now find books subgenre-first. Sharpen categories and keywords to the subtrope, not the genre — and if your series brushes folk horror or dystopia, 2027 is being set up for you. Books take months; the forecast is a head start.
