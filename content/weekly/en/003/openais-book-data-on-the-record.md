Briefs filed on September 17 in Alter v. OpenAI and Microsoft were unsealed, and the Authors Guild [summarised them on September 21](https://authorsguild.org/news/ag-v-openai-top-execs-knew-mass-book-piracy-was-illegal/). These are the plaintiffs’ allegations, not findings. They say Microsoft knew of LibGen use by April 2019, that LibGen files were deleted in summer 2022 under a “Project Clear,” and they quote a May 2020 statement attributed to Jack Clark: “The better we do on GPT-X, the more worried genre fiction authors will become about us substituting for them on Amazon.” More briefing is expected, with a hearing in early 2027.

Separately, the Guild’s [September 17 update](https://authorsguild.org/news/important-information-regarding-anthropic-copyright-settlement-claim-notices/) on the Anthropic settlement extends the window for co-claimants to settle how a work’s payment is split from 30 days to 60. Money for a disputed work is held until the dispute is resolved, first by the administrator and then, if needed, by a court-appointed Special Master.

:: If you share a claim with a co-author or a publisher, agree the split now. Every unresolved dispute is money that sits still.
