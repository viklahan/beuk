---
title: The Bookshops Abroad Are Running Short
section: The Wide World
byline: The Beuk Desk
deck: Argentina declares an emergency, Japan counts its empty towns, and Romania doubles the tax on a book
---
Carlo Carrenho’s [Around the Book World roundup](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/09/around-the-book-world-monday-september-21-2026/) in Publishing Perspectives on September 21 read like a report from the front. In Argentina the trade group FALPA has declared a nationwide bookstore emergency. Its survey of about 1,200 independent shops compared January to August 2026 with the same months of 2023: units down 25%, revenue adjusted for inflation down 41.5%, and real costs up 31.1%. FALPA wants the fixed-price law enforced and emergency financing, among other measures, and Fundación El Libro backs it.

Japan has fewer than 10,000 bookstores, and 510 municipalities had none at all as of March 2026. From October, Kodansha, Shogakukan and Shueisha start delivering direct to shops through PubteX. Meanwhile Poplar and Japan Post are selling children’s books at four post offices in Fukushima.

In Romania, VAT on books rose from 5% to 11%, and publishers at Curtea Veche and Nemira describe weaker demand and smaller print runs. Sweden shows a different pressure: Mediavision counts 9% fewer households with an audiobook subscription than a year ago, while households with access through Spotify nearly doubled, and daily reach held at about 15%.

:: “Going wide” in print means selling into shops like these, and they are buying less and paying more to stay open. If your print-on-demand price is set for one home market, check what it comes to abroad after tax. And the Swedish numbers suggest audio listening is moving into a bundle rather than growing.
