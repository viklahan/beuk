What you're doing: stepping back from the page to see the whole book — its length, its rhythm, who keeps walking on — without anything telling you what to fix. Everything here is counted on your device.

## Steps

1. Open **Story Insights**. It begins with **At a glance**: words, chapters, reading time, pages on your reading device, average sentence length and how much of the book is dialogue — the same counts the sidebar and the reading desk show.
2. Explore the charts. Hover or tab to any chapter in **Chapter rhythm**, **Cadence**, **Reading ease** or **Dialogue and narration** to see its words, sentence length and dialogue share; click it, or press Enter, to open that chapter.
3. Read **Names on stage**, the last section: one row per name the book keeps returning to, one column per section, each square shaded by how often the name appears there for the section's length. Choose a square and the studio opens that section with the first mention selected.
4. Open **Goals** to set a daily target, a project target or a deadline, and to see **The road so far**: the book's length over time.

## Explanation

### Where the counts come from

- **Reading time** uses 238 words a minute, everywhere in the studio. That is the average silent reading rate for adults reading English non-fiction in a 2019 review of 190 studies (Brysbaert, *Journal of Memory and Language*); fiction ran a little faster, at 260 a minute.
- **Pages** are the reading desk's own count for the device on the desk, the same number in the sidebar, on the desk and here. Until the desk has measured the book once, a rough 250 words a page stands in.
- **Reading ease** is an approximate Flesch score for each chapter, for English books. Higher scores mean shorter sentences and shorter words: it measures the shape of the prose, not its worth.
- **Words to look at** are the small filter and hedge words (felt, just, very, -ly…) with the chapters where they gather — offered as words to look at, never as mistakes.

### Names on stage

Names are found by capital letters, on this device — a rule of thumb, not a reading of the story. A name appears once it is used often enough, in at least two sections. Places and ships turn up too; hide any that aren't names, and they stay hidden with the book. Books in scripts without capital letters get a short note instead of a guess.

### The road so far

Goals draws the book's length as one quiet line, only on the days the studio actually saw it. Days it never saw stay as gaps; they are never guessed. A project target appears as a level line, and with a deadline a dashed line shows the steady pace from today that would land it — a pace, not a forecast. Hover, tap or use the arrow keys for any day's count.

## Troubleshooting

### What can go wrong

**The page count changed when I switched device.** Pages are counted for the device on the desk, and a phone holds fewer words to a page than a tablet. Words and reading time don't change.

**The road so far has no line yet.** The log began keeping the book's length on 26 October 2026, and a line needs two kept days.

**A place or a ship is in Names on stage.** Hide it from its row. The hidden list is kept with the book.

**There is no reading-ease chart.** It is offered for English books only; the formula was made for English.
