Harlan Voss took over the mill in Greybirch from his father when it employed forty men. Now it employs twelve, and he has signed a letter of intent: four point two million dollars for the mill, the lakeshore parcels and his point of land on Lake Annerin, closing in twelve days.

On festival Saturday, Tommy Beck motors down the lake at seven in the morning to pull Harlan's dock before the ice. The state medical examiner's toxicology comes back the next day: digoxin, five times the therapeutic level.

Police chief Silas Brennan has a small town where everybody knows everybody else's business, or thinks they do, and a dead mill owner whose own daughter told him people would hate him for selling.

> A town was a living thing, and this one was going thin at the edges like an old shirt.

Thirty chapters, set in October as the birches turn gold, with twenty full-page illustrations.
