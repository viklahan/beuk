The Red Arrow left Leningradsky Station at 23:55, the way it had every night for fifty years, and Babeetza Romanov watched it go from the platform with a ticket in a name she had been born with, which in Moscow was the most dangerous thing about her.

Babeetza is thirty-two, American, a freelance journalist on paper and a CIA officer under non-official cover in fact, rebuilding a burned network from zero. She inherits her predecessor's annotated copy of the Moscow Rules, a GRU major whose scarf colour is her signal, and a KGB colonel called the Hound who wants the third party more than he wants her.

The story runs from Moscow to Leningrad, Helsinki and both sides of Berlin, between April and August 1983.

> Assume hostile surveillance, never deviate from normal behavior, never look back — and Harrow's pencil marginalia asking normal for who?

Thirty chapters in five parts, a tradecraft glossary, a cast list, a sneak peek at Volume Two, and fifteen full-page illustrations.
