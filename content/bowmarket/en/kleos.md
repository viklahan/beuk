Twenty-four years before the fall of Troy, the gods come down the mountain in their gold to a wedding on Pelion. The groom is Peleus, a mortal king. The bride is the sea-goddess Thetis, given, not won: it had been spoken that the son she bore would be greater than his father.

The son is Achilles. On the shingle before the fleet sails for Troy, his mother lays two fates before him: a long life, obscure, unremembered, or glory undying and death at Troy.

> You chose glory. Glory has a price, and the price is you.

Thirty chapters, from the wedding feast through the quarrel, the embassy, the duel before the Scaean gates and the ransom of Hector, to the golden urn on the headland at Sigeum, with fifteen plates drawn in the manner of black-figure vase painting.
