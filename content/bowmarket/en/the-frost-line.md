The Frost Line follows Babeetza Romanov, a CIA officer working in Moscow under journalist cover, from April 1983 to the spring of 1984: from a burned network and a mole called Nightingale, through a hunt for the traitor inside her own station, to a bunker south of Moscow that does not officially exist.

Real events supply the historical spine: the Strategic Defense Initiative, the shooting-down of Korean Air Lines Flight 007, the early-warning false alarm of September 1983, and NATO's exercise Able Archer 83. The fiction lives in the gaps between what the capitals believed and what was happening.

> The tradecraft in this book — dead drops, brush passes, one-time pads, numbers stations — is real, though simplified for the page. The misunderstandings are the point.

The set holds all three books in story order, each with fifteen full-page illustrations, a tradecraft glossary and a cast list.
