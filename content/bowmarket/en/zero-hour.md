At 02:55 on a Tuesday morning, Babeetza Romanov sat in the dark of the Charlottenburg flat with Harrow's Panasonic on the kitchen table, and waited for 03:00. It was not a Thursday. That was the first wrong thing.

The numbers station never breaks schedule. When it does, the trail leads from West Berlin back east, to an early-warning command post south of Moscow: a room with no windows, where one duty officer decides whether a launch warning goes upstairs.

> This is a work of fiction, and the last of three. Babeetza Romanov, Anna Voronova, and everyone else in these pages never lived — but the winter did.

The September 1983 false alarm in which a Soviet officer chose not to believe his own warning system is real, and it is the shadow this book stands in. The malfunction at its centre is invented.

Thirty chapters in five parts, a tradecraft glossary, a cast list, a coda for the trilogy, and fifteen full-page illustrations.
