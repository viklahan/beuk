Ren runs Bar Kuro, six seats in Golden Gai, Shinjuku. He still counts his fingers every morning: four on the left hand, the stump of the little one healed to a pale cap of scar.

On a rainy Monday the news says Kurosawa Genji, seventy-one, the man who pulled him out of a Kabukicho alley at nineteen, was found dead in the Ginza Crest Tower. The certificate says a burst vessel. No foul play. For a man like the oyabun, it is too small a death.

> Debts don't go away because you stop looking at them, Ren. They wait.

Thirty chapters, from the wake to the docks, with twenty-six plates drawn in the manner of Japanese animated dark noir: rain, neon and smoke.
